About

I'm a researcher and engineer.

I'm currently an OpenAI Safety Fellow, exploring whether optimizing for calibration yields capability generalization, with a focus on domains that are economically viable or serve as social-good substrates. Any views I express, on this site or in any other public forum, are my own and do not represent OpenAI.

This summer, my curiosity about how reward models work led me to join a PRISM research fellowship group working on that topic, mentored by Rachel Freedman, a final-year PhD student at UC Berkeley's Center for Human-Compatible AI (CHAI). Our group wrote Reward Basis Decomposition for Interpreting Preference Heterogeneity, accepted to the NeurIPS 2026 Interpretability as a Science workshop on September 29, 2026. Mohamed Eldagla, Prerana Rane, and I are joint first authors.

Before that, I was an MS student in Asaf Cidon's group at Columbia, where I worked on Dances with Locks, an adaptive commit protocol for distributed transactions.

My summers during undergrad and grad school were internships, most recent first:

  • Apple, on the observability team, building LLM-powered tooling for petabyte-scale observability data
  • Figma, on the security infrastructure team
  • Intel, as a research fellow (media coverage)
  • Princeton, in the Princeton-Intel REU with Prof. Jennifer Rexford, on security and formal verification. This was my first summer doing research outside of school.

I have an MS in Computer Engineering from Columbia and a BS from UIUC. After graduating in May 2026, I chose fellowships over going directly into a PhD, to see what is happening at the edge of AI research.

Beyond the summer paper and the OpenAI fellowship, that choice has led to some interesting conversations. I've taken calls with people running engine block dealerships, and was invited to visit one of the leading dealerships, headquartered in Cairo. I'll likely take up that invitation next summer, to explore where my work could be useful: settings where decisions matter and can be documented by calibrated systems. For now, my time is going fully into the fellowship.
