About

I'm a researcher and engineer.

I'm currently an OpenAI Safety Fellow, exploring whether optimizing for calibration yields capability generalization, with a focus on domains that are economically viable or serve as social-good substrates.

This summer, my curiosity about how reward models work led to a paper, Reward Basis Decomposition for Interpreting Preference Heterogeneity, accepted to the NeurIPS 2026 Interpretability as a Science workshop on September 29, 2026. Mohamed Eldagla, Prerana Rane, and I are joint first authors. We wrote it through the PRISM research fellowship, mentored by Rachel Freedman, a final-year PhD student at UC Berkeley's Center for Human-Compatible AI (CHAI).

Before that, I was an MS student in Asaf Cidon's group at Columbia, where I worked on Dances with Locks, an adaptive commit protocol for distributed transactions.

My summers during undergrad and grad school were internships, most recent first:

  • Apple, on the observability team, building LLM-powered tooling for petabyte-scale observability data
  • Figma, on the security infrastructure team
  • Intel, as a research fellow (media coverage)
  • Princeton, in the Princeton-Intel REU with Jennifer Rexford, on security and formal verification. This was my first summer doing research outside of school.

I have an MS in Computer Engineering from Columbia and a BS from UIUC.
